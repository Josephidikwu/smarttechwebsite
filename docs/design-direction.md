# Smart Technology Website — Design & Art Direction Brief

> Source of truth for visual/composition decisions during build. Check every new section against
> this before considering it done — see the 12-point review at the end.

You are a senior brand designer, product designer, UX/UI designer and digital art director.

Design the Smart Technology website as a distinctive, premium 2026 technology brand experience.

The official Smart Technology brand guide (see `docs/brand-guide-page-*.png` and
`public/brand/`) is the primary source of truth for the logo, colours, typography, imagery,
spacing and visual identity. Do not invent or override the brand identity where the guide
provides specific rules.

The website is built with React and Next.js.

The design direction is MODERN, LIGHT, PREMIUM, SPACIOUS, EDITORIAL AND TECHNOLOGY-FOCUSED.

**IMPORTANT: do not make this website look like a generic AI-generated SaaS website.**

Avoid the common AI website-builder pattern: Hero banner → 3 rounded cards → gradient banner →
3 rounded cards → testimonial cards → large CTA banner → footer. Do not repeat this structure.

The goal is a website that looks intentionally designed by an experienced human product/brand
designer, not assembled from generic AI components.

## Core design principle

The website should feel: Modern, Light, Premium, Sophisticated, Confident, Spacious, Editorial,
Technological, Human, Distinctive, Purposeful.

Think in terms of:

- LESS UI. MORE DESIGN.
- LESS CARDS. MORE COMPOSITION.
- LESS DECORATION. MORE HIERARCHY.
- LESS TEMPLATE. MORE IDENTITY.

## Do not overuse cards

Do NOT put every piece of content inside a card. Cards should only be used when the content
genuinely benefits from being presented as a discrete object. Do not make every section a grid of
rounded cards.

Avoid: cards everywhere; excessive rounded containers/shadows/borders; uniform 24px/32px radius on
everything; floating cards for no reason; glassmorphism everywhere; dashboard cards floating
around the hero; cards stacked inside cards; every service/statistic/CTA becoming a card.

If content can be communicated better through typography, whitespace, imagery or composition, use
that instead.

## Do not overuse banners

Avoid "banneritis". Do not create large coloured rounded rectangles simply to separate sections.
Do not end every section with a giant CTA banner. Sections should flow through whitespace,
typography, scale, background transitions, imagery, subtle dividers, layout changes, visual
rhythm. A full-width banner should only be used when it has genuine storytelling or visual
purpose.

## Avoid generic AI heroes

Do NOT use: huge heading + paragraph + two pill buttons + gradient blob + floating UI cards +
abstract 3D shapes.

Instead: strong typography, product imagery, carefully art-directed technology imagery, editorial
composition, large negative space, subtle motion, cropping, architectural layout, product/
technology visual storytelling. Confident and sophisticated, not noisy.

## Layout variety

Every major section needs its own visual identity. Do NOT repeatedly use: heading + paragraph +
three cards + button for every section.

Vary compositions: large typography with significant whitespace; asymmetrical 40/60 layouts;
50/50 image and text; full-width imagery; editorial two-column layouts; large typographic
statements; product-focused layouts; horizontal product/category presentations; full-bleed
imagery; dark immersive technology sections; minimal sections with almost no UI; overlapping
image/text compositions; large visual focal points; vertical editorial layouts; split-screen
sections; carefully controlled horizontal scrolling.

The website should feel like a continuous visual narrative, not a collection of unrelated
components.

## Smart Technology visual language

Smart Technology is not simply a SaaS company — it represents a broader technology ecosystem:
technology products (laptops, computers, headphones, accessories, networking), procurement,
software, AI, data, IT infrastructure, digital solutions, training, internship, careers.

Combine: TECHNOLOGY + PRODUCTS + INNOVATION + BUSINESS + HUMAN OPPORTUNITY. Different sections
communicate different aspects:

- **Product sections:** premium technology/product editorial design — strong product imagery,
  whitespace, typography, clean product presentation.
- **Software/AI sections:** technical, innovative, forward-looking — subtle data visualisation,
  motion, typography, carefully designed digital imagery where appropriate.
- **Training/Internship/Careers:** human, optimistic, aspirational — not generic HR pages.
- **Corporate/About:** confident, mature, credible.

## Typography

Typography is one of the main design tools. Use strong hierarchy. Large headlines should have
enough scale and whitespace for visual impact. Do not compensate for weak typography with more
cards, gradients or decoration. Use typography for hierarchy, rhythm, contrast, visual pauses,
section transitions, brand personality. Contemporary and refined. Use the brand guide's fonts;
where unspecified, a modern, highly legible typeface appropriate for a premium technology
company (see plan: Plus Jakarta Sans stand-in until the real typeface is confirmed).

## Whitespace

Generous, intentional whitespace: create hierarchy, separate ideas, give imagery room to breathe,
emphasise headlines, improve readability, make the site feel premium. A section doesn't need a
visual element just because there's empty space.

## Colour

Follow the Smart Technology brand guide. Use the official brand colours consistently. Prefer a
light visual foundation unless the guide indicates otherwise: white, off-white, very light neutral
backgrounds, brand colours, dark typography, carefully controlled accent colours. Dark sections
used strategically for AI, technology, innovation, infrastructure, major visual moments — do not
make the entire website dark. Gradients only when they genuinely support the brand identity, never
just because they look "modern".

## Imagery

Avoid generic corporate stock photography: people shaking hands, random office meetings, generic
business people staring at laptops, fake "technology team" imagery, overused blue digital network
backgrounds.

Prefer: high-quality technology product photography, carefully art-directed technology imagery,
realistic workplace technology, African/modern business context where appropriate, laptops and
devices, human interaction with technology, abstract but purposeful technology visuals, data/AI
visualisations, editorial photography. Imagery should feel intentional and premium.

## Product design

Smart Technology sells laptops, headphones, accessories, networking equipment and other technology
products — the catalogue should feel like a serious technology retail experience, not generic
ecommerce cards.

Use large product imagery, clean backgrounds, strong product typography, editorial product
layouts, category navigation, minimal UI. Phase 1 is not an online store — product pages focus on
discovery and enquiry, not checkout. Use "Explore Product" / "Request Product" / "Request a
Quote", never "Add to Cart". The architecture should still be ready for future ecommerce
functionality.

## Our Companies section

Represents Oracle Digital Infor Hub Ltd alongside Smart Technology as part of a broader technology
ecosystem. Do not present as two generic company cards — use a sophisticated editorial
presentation (typography, visual separation, logos, imagery, editorial split layout). The
relationship should read as a broader technology ecosystem, e.g.:

```
SMART TECHNOLOGY            ORACLE DIGITAL
Technology Products &       AI, Software & Digital
Digital Solutions           Innovation
```

Do not claim Oracle Digital is legally a subsidiary unless that relationship is separately
confirmed.

## Opportunities section

Training, Internship and Careers matter to the brand. Do not present as three generic rounded
cards. Create an editorial, human section — large statement ("Learn. Experience. Build.") then a
strong typographic layout introducing each area through hierarchy, typography, imagery and
whitespace rather than three identical cards.

## Motion

Subtle, purposeful motion only: page entrance, scroll reveals, image transitions, hover states,
product interactions, navigation, micro-interactions, subtle parallax where appropriate. Do not
animate everything. Avoid constant floating objects, bouncing elements, excessive parallax,
distracting or delaying animation, decorative motion with no purpose. Support
`prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Buttons

Clean and intentional — avoid making every button a huge pill. Use the brand's button style.
Combine primary button, secondary button, text link where possible. Don't put multiple large
buttons everywhere; use contextual CTAs (Explore Solutions, Explore Products, Request a Product,
Request a Quote, Discuss Your Project, Apply for Training, Apply for Internship, View Careers, Get
Started).

## Navigation

Minimal and premium: Home, About, Solutions, Products, Opportunities, Our Companies, Insights,
Contact — plus one prominent but restrained primary CTA ("Get Started"). Do not overcrowd. Clean,
polished mobile navigation.

## Visual rhythm

Alternate between dense information, spacious visual moments, typography, imagery, products,
technology, human stories. Avoid uniform density throughout. Create pause moments. Use large
headlines as visual anchors.

## Design review rule

After designing the homepage (and every subsequent section), review it specifically for
AI-generated design patterns:

1. Are there too many cards?
2. Are there too many rounded rectangles?
3. Are there too many banners?
4. Are gradients being overused?
5. Is the same 3-column layout repeated?
6. Does every section have a CTA?
7. Does every section have a coloured background?
8. Are there unnecessary decorative elements?
9. Does the hero look like a generic SaaS template?
10. Does the website have enough whitespace?
11. Does each section feel visually distinct?
12. Does the website have a recognisable Smart Technology identity?

If any answer indicates repetition, redesign the relevant section before continuing.

## Final art direction

The final website should feel like: a serious technology company; a modern technology products
business; a digital innovation company; a place where people can learn and build careers.
Appropriate for a company operating in Africa and the global digital economy, without relying on
stereotypical "African technology" visuals.

- LIGHT, NOT EMPTY.
- MINIMAL, NOT BORING.
- PREMIUM, NOT OVERDESIGNED.
- MODERN, NOT TREND-CHASING.
- TECHNOLOGICAL, NOT ROBOTIC.
- HUMAN, NOT CORPORATE.
- DISTINCTIVE, NOT TEMPLATE-BASED.

Most importantly: **do not design a collection of cards. Design a brand experience.**
